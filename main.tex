\begin{abstract}
Hierarchical multi-label classification (HMC) requires predictions to be
consistent with a class hierarchy: an ancestor must not score lower than its
descendants. Prior work embeds this taxonomic prior into coupled neural
architectures, joint training objectives such as max-constraint loss (MCLoss),
or ontology graph autoencoders. Four fundamental questions have remained
unresolved: (1) whether the empirical benefit of R-matrix constraints stems from
training-time consistency losses or inference-time bottom-up reconciliation;
(2) how exact ancestor-closure constraints can scale to large directed acyclic
graphs (DAGs) without quadratic $O(C^2)$ memory; (3) which structural properties
determine when reconciliation helps; and (4) why standard ontology graph
autoencoders collapse sibling classes and how ontology geometry, hierarchical
multi-label contrastive learning, and depth calibration can be unified with
exact reconciliation. Using \texttt{hmc-torch}, we first conduct a $3 \times 2$
factorial evaluation of three training objectives (BCE, hierarchical hinge, and
MCLoss) crossed with raw versus reconciled inference across 27 benchmarks
spanning four domain families (scientific/legal text, FunCat, Gene Ontology, and
cross-domain ARFF) under a strict validation-only protocol. Across all 27
datasets, inference-time reconciliation drives the empirical gains (Wilcoxon
signed-rank $p = 2.1\times 10^{-7}$ on Micro-AUPRC, improving 26/27 datasets;
$p = 1.3\times 10^{-6}$ on Micro-F1, improving 25/27 datasets), whereas
training-time consistency losses alone provide no consistent gain over plain BCE
and cause raw pre-reconciliation scores under MCLoss to collapse. Reconciliation
gain correlates significantly with the average score raise on internal nodes
(Spearman $\rho = 0.49$, $p = 0.009$) rather than static positive-label density
($\rho = -0.07$). To scale to large ontologies (up to 4,520 hierarchy nodes,
4,519 evaluated, and 5,839 edges), we introduce \texttt{BlockDiagonalR}, a sparse
$O(C{+}E)$ graph formulation that guarantees zero hierarchy violations with
$>1{,}400\times$ less memory than the dense $O(C^2)$ R-matrix. Combined with
\texttt{SR-GAE} and \texttt{H-MLCL+H-KNN}, it is statistically tied with C-HMCNN
on FunCat (0.263 vs.\ 0.264 AUPRC), matches C-HMCNN on Gene Ontology (0.421 vs.\
0.420, winning on 5/8 datasets) while 0.001 below HMCN-F (0.422), and improves
cross-domain benchmarks (0.859 vs.\ 0.849 AUPRC); a fine-tuned SPECTER2
(globalE2E) reaches 0.8704 Micro-F1 on Web of Science. Finally, triggered by an
independent reproducibility audit of Surj, we derive a Bessel-type bound
($\cos(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_p, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{c_i}) \le 1/\sqrt{K}$) explaining
why standard ontology GAEs collapse sibling embeddings (median cosine up to
0.9995), and introduce the \textbf{Sibling-Repulsive Graph Autoencoder
(\texttt{SR-GAE})}, \textbf{Hierarchical Multi-Label Contrastive Learning with
$k$-NN retrieval (\texttt{H-MLCL+H-KNN})}, and
\textbf{Consistency-Preserving Depth Calibration (\texttt{DepthCal})}.
\texttt{SR-GAE}+\texttt{H-MLCL+H-KNN} reduces the median sibling cosine to
$\le 0.038$ and strictly improves test Micro-AUPRC over the matched baseline on
27/27 benchmarks over 3 seeds (Wilcoxon $p = 1.5 \times 10^{-8}$; 26/27 strict
wins in controlled single-stage ablation, $p = 4.5 \times 10^{-8}$); adding
\texttt{DepthCal} lifts global test F1 on 26/27 benchmarks ($p = 2.5 \times
10^{-6}$; 26/27 strict, 27/27 non-decreasing, $p = 3.0 \times 10^{-8}$ in
controlled ablation) and deep-level F1 by $+13.0\%$ on FunCat and $+24.1\%$ on
GO Level 5, with zero hierarchy violations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Hierarchical Multi-Label Classification (HMC) is a knowledge-constrained
structured prediction task in which each instance is assigned to one or more
nodes in a predefined class taxonomy organized as a tree or a directed acyclic
graph (DAG)~\cite{silla2011hmc}. HMC is central to knowledge-based systems
across scientific and industrial domains, including categorizing documents
into scientific or legal taxonomies (ArXiv, Web of Science, EUR-Lex),
annotating genes and proteins with functional ontologies (FunCat, Gene
Ontology), and classifying biological specimens or medical images into
morphological hierarchies. Every valid assignment in HMC must satisfy the
\emph{true-path rule} (or hierarchical consistency constraint): whenever a
class is active, all of its ancestors in the taxonomy must also be active. In
probabilistic terms, the predicted score $p_i$ of any ancestor class $i$ must
be at least as large as the score $p_j$ of any of its descendants $j$
($p_i \geq p_j$).

Over the past decade, the HMC literature has proposed increasingly elaborate
mechanisms to inject this taxonomic knowledge into neural classifiers---ranging
from recurrent level-wise propagation in HMCN~\cite{weihs2018hmc} and graph
convolutional networks or hierarchy-aware transformers in text classification
(HiAGM~\cite{zhou2020hiagm}, HTCInfoMax~\cite{htcinfomax},
HGCLR~\cite{hgclr}, HPT~\cite{hpt}) to neuro-symbolic constraint layers such
as the \textbf{R-matrix} (ancestor-closure matrix) in C-HMCNN~\cite{chmcnn}
and ontology graph autoencoders in Surj~\cite{www2022ontology}. However, in
nearly all prior studies, the hierarchical constraint is evaluated as an
indivisible package bundling a specific backbone architecture, a training-time
consistency objective (such as max-constraint loss, MCLoss), and an
inference-time max-propagation step. Consequently, four fundamental scientific
questions remain unanswered:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,label=\textbf{Q\arabic*.}]
  \item \textbf{Training vs.\ Inference Decomposition:} Does the empirical
    benefit of the R-matrix constraint stem from shaping gradient updates via
    a consistency loss during training, or from enforcing structural coherence
    via bottom-up reconciliation at inference time?
  \item \textbf{Scalability to Large DAGs:} How can exact ancestor-closure
    reconciliation and constraint training be scaled to deep, multi-parent
    ontologies with thousands of classes without incurring the quadratic
    $O(C^2)$ memory and $O(B \cdot C^2)$ compute cost of a dense R-matrix?
  \item \textbf{Structural Predictors of Constraint Gain:} Which structural
    and predictive properties of a hierarchy explain why reconciliation yields
    substantial gains on some benchmarks while remaining nearly neutral on
    others?
  \item \textbf{Ontology Graph Geometry, Contrastive Representation Learning,
    and Depth Calibration:} Why do standard ontology graph autoencoders
    collapse sibling classes onto a single ray, and how can sibling-repulsive
    ontology representations, R-matrix multi-label contrastive learning, and
    consistency-preserving depth calibration be unified with bottom-up
    reconciliation to improve both global AUPRC and deep-level F1?
\end{enumerate}

To answer these questions rigorously, we decoupled the R-matrix constraint
from any single backbone architecture within \texttt{hmc-torch}, a controlled
experimental framework supporting text, genomic, biological, and imaging
modalities under a unified validation-only evaluation protocol. Throughout the
paper, we distinguish between the \textbf{R-matrix constraint} (the
hierarchical consistency property $\forall (i,j) \in \text{ancestor}: p_i \geq
p_j$), the \textbf{dense R-matrix} (the explicit $C \times C$ binary ancestor
closure matrix), and our \textbf{sparse R-matrix formulation}
(\texttt{BlockDiagonalR}, which enforces the exact same semantics over graph
edges in $O(C{+}E)$ space).

Our main contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Factorial Decomposition of Training vs.\ Inference Constraints
    (Q1):} Across 27 benchmarks spanning four domain families (text, FunCat,
    Gene Ontology, and cross-domain ARFF), we evaluate a $3 \times 2$
    factorial design crossing three training objectives---unconstrained binary
    cross-entropy (BCE), BCE with a hierarchical hinge penalty, and
    MCLoss---with raw versus reconciled inference. We demonstrate that
    inference-time reconciliation is the primary driver of performance gains
    (Wilcoxon signed-rank $p = 2.1\times 10^{-7}$ on Micro-AUPRC across 26/27
    datasets; $p = 1.3\times 10^{-6}$ on Micro-F1 across 25/27 datasets),
    whereas training-time consistency penalties alone do not reliably improve
    upon plain BCE and can cause raw pre-reconciliation scores under MCLoss to
    collapse.
  \item \textbf{Sparse $O(C{+}E)$ R-Matrix Formulation for Large DAGs (Q2):}
    We formalize \texttt{BlockDiagonalR}, an edge-indexed sparse propagation
    and constraint layer that replaces the $O(C^2)$ dense ancestor closure
    matrix with $O(C{+}E)$ storage and $O(B \cdot E)$ bottom-up
    scatter-reduction. On Gene Ontology DAGs with up to $C{=}4{,}134$ nodes
    (4,130 evaluated) and $E{=}5{,}839$ edges (and EUR-Lex with $C{=}4{,}520$ nodes),
    \texttt{BlockDiagonalR} guarantees zero hierarchy violations while reducing
    structural memory by over $1{,}400\times$ (65~MB to 46~KB) and enabling
    large-batch training where dense $R$ exhausts GPU memory.
  \item \textbf{Structural Analysis of Reconciliation Regimes (Q3):} We show
    that the magnitude of the reconciliation gain across all 27 datasets is
    not explained by the static fraction of positive labels on internal nodes
    (Spearman $\rho = -0.07$, $p = 0.721$), but correlates significantly with
    the average score raise induced on internal nodes during bottom-up
    propagation ($\rho = 0.49$, $p = 0.009$).
  \item \textbf{Cross-Benchmark Comparison and Reproducibility Audit of
    Surj:} Using a strict validation-only threshold selection protocol,
    \texttt{SR-GAE + H-MLCL+H-KNN + DepthCal} is statistically tied with
    C-HMCNN on FunCat (0.263 vs.\ 0.264 AUPRC) and above HMCN-F (0.261),
    matches C-HMCNN on Gene Ontology (0.421 vs.\ 0.420 average AUPRC, winning
    on 5/8 datasets) while 0.001 below HMCN-F (0.422), and improves on
    cross-domain benchmarks (0.859 vs.\ 0.849 AUPRC; $+3.0$~pp on
    \texttt{diatoms}); it reaches 0.7247 / 0.7489 / 0.8232 / 0.7893 Micro-F1 on
    frozen SPECTER2 text benchmarks, and a fine-tuned SPECTER2 (globalE2E)
    achieves 0.8704 Micro-F1 on Web of Science (matching our 3-seed re-execution
    of HPT at $0.8691 \pm 0.0023$). Furthermore, our independent reimplementation
    and audit of Surj~\cite{www2022ontology} across 10 seeds and 25 validation
    configurations demonstrates that its reported 5.5--29\% gains over C-HMCNN
    (up to 31\% over the stronger baseline) cannot be verified from the
    published specification.
  \item \textbf{Sibling-Repulsive Ontology Graphs (\texttt{SR-GAE}),
    Hierarchical Multi-Label Contrastive Learning (\texttt{H-MLCL+H-KNN}), and
    Depth Calibration (\texttt{DepthCal}) (Q4):} We derive a Bessel-type bound
    ($\cos(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_p, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{c_i}) \le 1/\sqrt{K}$) and a
    Rankin--Welch packing regime ($K_{\max} > D$) explaining why standard
    ontology GAEs collapse sibling embeddings (median cosine up to 0.9995). To
    resolve both horizontal sibling collapse and vertical score decay, we
    introduce \texttt{SR-GAE}, \texttt{H-MLCL+H-KNN} (sibling-boosted prototype
    InfoNCE + FIFO queue R-matrix Jaccard instance contrast + $k$-NN retrieval),
    and \texttt{DepthCal} ($L$ per-depth logit offsets prior to
    \texttt{BlockDiagonalR}). Across the 27 benchmarks (3 seeds),
    \texttt{SR-GAE}+\texttt{H-MLCL+H-KNN} reduces the median sibling cosine to
    $\le 0.038$, improves cosine-head test AUPRC on 10/10 FunCat ($+6.9\%$ mean,
    up to $+22.7\%$), 6/9 GO (up to $+8.6\%$), and text taxonomies ($+20.4\%$ on
    ArXiv, $+25.8\%$ on WOS), and strictly improves test Micro-AUPRC over the
    matched baseline on \textbf{27/27 datasets} (Wilcoxon $p = 1.5 \times
    10^{-8}$; 26/27 strict wins in controlled single-stage ablation, $p = 4.5
    \times 10^{-8}$); adding \texttt{DepthCal} lifts test F1 on \textbf{26/27
    datasets} ($p = 2.5 \times 10^{-6}$; 26/27 strict, 27/27 non-decreasing,
    $p = 3.0 \times 10^{-8}$ in controlled ablation) as well as deep-level F1 on
    10/10 FunCat datasets ($+13.0\%$ mean) and 8/9 GO datasets ($+24.1\%$ mean
    on Level 5) with zero hierarchy violations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Local, Global, and Hierarchy-Aware HMC Architectures}

Early HMC methods decomposed the taxonomy into local classifiers trained per
node, per parent, or per level~\cite{silla2011hmc,cerri2014hmc}, exemplified
by decision-tree ensembles in CLUS-HMC~\cite{clus} and standardized software
libraries such as HiClass~\cite{miranda2023hiclass}. Because local classifiers
make independent decisions, their predictions frequently violate the true-path
rule unless post-processed. Global methods address this by predicting all
classes jointly within a single neural model: HMCN~\cite{weihs2018hmc}
combines local level-specific outputs with a global feed-forward (HMCN-F) or
recurrent (HMCN-R) highway across hierarchy depths.

In hierarchical text classification (HTC), recent methods focus on injecting
the label taxonomy into pretrained language models. HiAGM~\cite{zhou2020hiagm}
couples a text encoder with a bidirectional Tree-LSTM or Graph Convolutional
Network (GCN) over the label graph; HTCInfoMax~\cite{htcinfomax} augments
this architecture with text--label mutual information maximization; and
HiMatch~\cite{himatch} formulates HTC as semantic matching in a joint
embedding space. More recent transformer-based approaches integrate graph
structure directly into the language backbone: HGCLR~\cite{hgclr} uses
hierarchy-guided contrastive learning to align text representations with
positive label subgraphs, HBGL~\cite{jiang2022hbgl} injects global and local
hierarchies directly into BERT attention masks, HPT~\cite{hpt} casts HTC as
hierarchy-aware prompt tuning with level-wise virtual tokens, and
HJCL~\cite{u2023hjcl} introduces instance-wise and label-wise contrastive
objectives. Most recently, contrastive learning has been further specialized for
hierarchical text and image taxonomies: HTC-CLIP~\cite{htcclip2023} generates
path-guided positive samples with a structural encoder, HILL~\cite{hill2024}
minimizes structural entropy on a hierarchy coding tree for information-lossless
contrastive learning, MLCL-KNN~\cite{mlclknn2024} couples multi-label
contrastive learning with $k$-nearest-neighbor retrieval at inference,
HCL4QC~\cite{hcl4qc2023} models intra- and inter-class hierarchy contrast for
short e-commerce queries, and HiMulConE~\cite{himulcone2022} scales pairwise
instance contrastive penalties by tree distance in computer vision. While
effective when rich label text or large sample batches are available, these
methods tightly couple hierarchy encoding to domain-specific backbones, incur
$O(B^2)$ batch contrastive overhead or synthetic sample generation, and do not
enforce hard taxonomic consistency ($\text{GHV} = 0$) on output probabilities.

\subsection{Neuro-Symbolic Constraints and the R-Matrix}

To guarantee logically consistent predictions across arbitrary domains,
Giunchiglia \& Lukasiewicz~\cite{chmcnn} introduced Coherent Hierarchical
Multi-Label Classification Networks (C-HMCNN) and the \textbf{R-matrix}
(ancestor-closure matrix), an $C \times C$ binary matrix where $R_{ij} = 1$ if
and only if class $i$ is an ancestor of class $j$ (including $i=j$). Placed on
top of a base neural network, the max-constraint module enforces
$\forall (i,j): R_{ij}=1 \implies p_i^{\text{rec}} \geq p_j^{\text{rec}}$ and
trains the network with max-constraint loss (MCLoss), which alters gradient
flow by routing ancestor supervision through descendant scores. This paradigm
was recently generalized to broader propositional logic constraints in
Coherent Concept Networks (CCN+)~\cite{giunchiglia2024ccn}. For functional
genomics (FunCat and Gene Ontology), Surj~\cite{www2022ontology} proposed
learning ontology representations via a graph autoencoder and scoring classes
by cosine similarity prior to R-matrix reconciliation, reporting substantial
AUPRC improvements over C-HMCNN; however, no reference implementation was
released, motivating our reproducibility audit in Section~\ref{sec:surj}.

Despite the widespread adoption of C-HMCNN, prior work treats the R-matrix
module and MCLoss as a monolithic unit, materializes the dense $C \times C$
ancestor matrix in memory, and leaves the geometric failure modes of ontology
graph autoencoders unexplored. We advance this line of work in four ways:
(1) we decouple training-time consistency penalties from inference-time
reconciliation across 27 benchmarks to isolate where the empirical benefit
originates; (2) we introduce \texttt{BlockDiagonalR}, an exact sparse
$O(C{+}E)$ formulation that eliminates the quadratic memory bottleneck on
large DAGs; (3) we characterize the structural conditions under which
reconciliation improves classification performance; and (4) we unify
sibling-repulsive ontology geometry (\texttt{SR-GAE}), R-matrix multi-label
contrastive learning with $k$-NN retrieval (\texttt{H-MLCL+H-KNN}), and
consistency-preserving depth calibration (\texttt{DepthCal}) with exact
bottom-up reconciliation.

\section{Methodology: Sparse R-Matrix Constraints, Sibling-Repulsive Ontology Graphs, and Contrastive Learning}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

To isolate the effect of hierarchical constraints and ontology representations
from confounding variations in data preprocessing, feature extraction, and
threshold selection, our methodology is organized around four design
principles: (1) \textbf{strict separation of concerns} via typed data
contracts, (2) \textbf{explicit tree versus DAG hierarchy modeling} with both
dense and sparse $O(C{+}E)$ constraint layers, (3) a \textbf{factorial
decomposition of training objectives and inference reconciliation} across
modalities, and (4) \textbf{horizontal sibling orthogonalization, R-matrix
multi-label contrastive learning, and vertical depth calibration} prior to
bottom-up reconciliation.

\subsection{Typed Data Contracts and Hierarchy Representations}

Every benchmark in our study is represented as a self-contained
\texttt{DatasetBundle} comprising three formal abstractions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{\texttt{Split}}: A dataset partition (train, validation, or
    test) containing a feature matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D}$, a
    global binary label matrix $Y \in \{0,1\}^{N \times C}$ over all $C$
    taxonomy nodes, per-level label slices
    $Y_{\text{local}} = [\mathbf{y}^{(0)}, \ldots, \mathbf{y}^{(L-1)}]$ across
    $L$ hierarchy depths, and sample identifiers for deduplication verification.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{Hierarchy}}: A formal representation of the class
    taxonomy as either a single-parent \texttt{TreeHierarchy} (e.g., FunCat,
    WOS, ArXiv) or a multi-parent \texttt{DagHierarchy} (e.g., Gene Ontology).
    Both expose a unified graph interface---\texttt{nodes}, \texttt{levels},
    \texttt{parents(child)}, \texttt{children(parent)},
    \texttt{ancestors(node)}, and the ancestor closure $R$---preventing the
    silent application of tree-only assumptions to multi-parent DAGs.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{FeatureMetadata}}: Specifies the input modality
    (tabular, text, biological expression, or vision), dimensionality, missing
    value profile, and whether raw inputs are available for end-to-end
    backbone fine-tuning.
\end{enumerate}

By standardizing all 27 datasets behind the \texttt{Split} and
\texttt{Hierarchy} contracts, any combination of feature encoder,
classification head, training objective, and reconciliation operator can be
evaluated under identical experimental conditions.

\subsection{Feature Encoders and Classification Heads}

Feature preprocessing follows a strict two-step \texttt{fit} $\rightarrow$
\texttt{transform} protocol on the training split so that scaling and
imputation statistics are estimated exclusively on training data without test
leakage:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Tabular and Biological Encoders
    (\texttt{TabularFeatureEncoder}):} Applies training-split mean imputation
    and z-score standardization, with optional variance-threshold filtering for
    high-dimensional tabular inputs ($>500$ features).
  \item \textbf{Text Encoders (\texttt{TextFeatureEncoder} and E2E Backbones):}
    Extracts 768-dimensional document representations from a pretrained
    scientific language model (\texttt{SPECTER2-base}~\cite{specter2} with CLS
    pooling), supporting both frozen feature extraction and end-to-end (E2E)
    transformer fine-tuning with differential learning rates.
\end{itemize}

Downstream of the encoder, we evaluate a shared global multi-layer perceptron
(\textbf{GlobalSigmoidHead}), $\text{MLP}(\mathbf{x}) \rightarrow \sigma(\cdot)
\rightarrow [0,1]^C$, which predicts unconstrained scores for all $C$ classes
jointly, alongside a \textbf{LocalLevelHead} baseline that trains independent
per-level MLPs ($\text{MLP}_\ell(\mathbf{x}) \rightarrow [0,1]^{C_\ell}$)
assembled via depth indexing.

\subsection{The R-Matrix Constraint: Training vs.\ Inference Modes}
\label{sec:rmatrix}

Given a class hierarchy with directed parent--child adjacency matrix $A \in
\{0,1\}^{C \times C}$ (where $A_{ij}=1$ if node $i$ is a direct child of node
$j$), the transitive ancestor closure matrix $R \in \{0,1\}^{C \times C}$~\cite{chmcnn}
is defined as:
\begin{equation}
R_{ij} = \begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } i = j \text{ or } \exists \text{ directed path } j \rightarrow \ldots \rightarrow i \text{ from child to ancestor,} \\
0 & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:rmatrix}
\end{equation}
For example, in the ArXiv taxonomy, the leaf label \texttt{cs.AI} links to
\texttt{cs} and \texttt{cs} links to \texttt{root}, yielding a two-hop path
$\texttt{cs.AI} \rightarrow \texttt{cs} \rightarrow \texttt{root}$ and setting
$R_{\text{root},\,\texttt{cs.AI}} = 1$. The closure $R$ thus encodes all
reflexive and transitive ancestor--descendant pairs $(i,j)$ for which the
true-path rule requires $p_i \geq p_j$.

To disentangle how this structural knowledge affects learning and prediction,
we expose the R-matrix constraint in two distinct, separable operating modes:

\textbf{Mode 1: Training-time consistency penalty.} During optimization, given
unconstrained per-class probabilities $p_{b,i} \in [0,1]$ for sample $b$ in a
mini-batch of size $B$, we can penalize hierarchy violations where a descendant
$j$ scores higher than its ancestor $i$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{hier}} = \frac{1}{B \cdot |\mathcal{P}|}
\sum_{b=1}^{B} \sum_{(i,j) \in \mathcal{P}}
\max\left(0,\; p_{b,j} - p_{b,i} + \gamma\right),
\label{eq:hierloss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{P} = \{(i,j) : R_{ij}=1,\, i \neq j\}$ is the set of
ancestor--descendant pairs (or the direct edge set $\mathcal{E}$ in the sparse
formulation below) and $\gamma \geq 0$ is an optional margin ($\gamma=0$ in all
experiments).

\textbf{Mode 2: Inference-time bottom-up reconciliation.} At evaluation time,
hard taxonomic consistency is guaranteed by propagating maxima bottom-up from
leaves to roots across hierarchy levels:
\begin{equation}
p_i^{\text{rec}} = \max_{j:\, R_{ij}=1} p_j
= \max\left(p_i,\; \max_{j \in \text{children}(i)} p_j^{\text{rec}}\right).
\label{eq:reconcile}
\end{equation}
Processing nodes in reverse topological (bottom-up breadth-first) order ensures
that $\forall (i,j) \text{ with } R_{ij}=1,\; p_i^{\text{rec}} \geq
p_j^{\text{rec}}$, resulting in strictly zero post-inference hierarchy
violations regardless of the training objective or encoder architecture.

\subsection{Sparse R-Matrix (\texttt{BlockDiagonalR}): Scaling to Large DAGs}
\label{sec:sparse}

A major scalability bottleneck of the dense R-matrix is its $O(C^2)$ storage
footprint and $O(B \cdot C^2)$ broadcast tensor product $(B, 1, C) \times
(1, C, C)$ during batch reconciliation and MCLoss computation. On Gene Ontology
DAGs with $C > 4{,}100$ classes, materializing the intermediate $(B, C, C)$
tensor requires $4.3$~GB of GPU memory at batch size $B{=}64$ and triggers
out-of-memory failures at larger batch sizes.

We overcome this bottleneck with \texttt{BlockDiagonalR}, a sparse graph-based
formulation that operates directly on the $E$ directed child$\rightarrow$parent
edges $\mathcal{E} = \{(c_e, p_e)\}_{e=1}^{E}$ of the hierarchy
(Figures~\ref{fig:sparse_graph} and~\ref{fig:sparse_flow}). Instead of storing
$R \in \{0,1\}^{C \times C}$, \texttt{BlockDiagonalR} stores only two flat
integer index vectors $\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{p} \in \{1,\ldots,C\}^E$ requiring
$O(C{+}E)$ space ($2E \times 4$ bytes). Given a batch of raw scores
$\mathbf{P}^{(0)} = \mathbf{P} \in [0,1]^{B \times C}$ and hierarchy depth $D$,
we iterate $d = 1, \ldots, D$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{P}^{(d)}_{b, i} = \max\Big(\mathbf{P}^{(d-1)}_{b, i},\;
\max_{e:\, p_e = i} \mathbf{P}^{(d-1)}_{b, c_e}\Big),
\label{eq:sparse_prop}
\end{equation}
implemented on GPU via vectorized parallel \texttt{scatter\_reduce} with
\texttt{amax} reduction over the edge index vectors $\mathbf{c}$ and
$\mathbf{p}$, terminating early at step $d \leq D$ as soon as
$\mathbf{P}^{(d)} = \mathbf{P}^{(d-1)}$. By the transitivity of $\max$ over
directed paths of length at most $D$, $\mathbf{P}^{(D)}$ is mathematically
identical to the dense closure $\max_{j: R_{ij}=1} P_{b,j}$, achieving
\textbf{zero hierarchy violations} in $O(B \cdot E)$ memory per step. Similarly,
because any ancestor--descendant violation along a path implies at least one
violation on a direct edge $(c_e, p_e) \in \mathcal{E}$, the sparse hinge
penalty evaluates Eq.~\eqref{eq:hierloss} directly over $\mathcal{P} =
\mathcal{E}$ in $O(B \cdot E)$ time. For \texttt{cellcycle\_GO}
($C{=}4{,}126$, $E{=}5{,}827$), \texttt{BlockDiagonalR} reduces structural
storage from 65~MB to 46.6~KB ($>1{,}400\times$ reduction) and peak batch
memory from $O(B \cdot C^2)$ to $O(B \cdot E)$.

\subsection{Factorial Training Objectives: BCE, Hinge, and MCLoss}

To answer Research Question Q1, we evaluate three distinct training objectives
paired with either raw ($\mathbf{P}$) or reconciled ($\mathbf{P}^{\text{rec}}$)
inference scores:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Unconstrained Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE):} Trains each class
    with standard binary cross-entropy (optionally weighted by class prevalence
    $w_i = N_{\text{neg}}^{(i)} / N_{\text{pos}}^{(i)}$ on imbalanced text
    corpora):
    \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(\mathbf{P}, \mathbf{Y}) = -\frac{1}{B \cdot C}
    \sum_{b=1}^{B} \sum_{i=1}^{C} w_i \left[
    y_{b,i} \log p_{b,i} + (1-y_{b,i}) \log(1-p_{b,i})
    \right].
    \label{eq:bce}
    \end{equation}
    Under BCE, the network receives no explicit cross-node penalty during
    training; hierarchical consistency is enforced purely at inference time via
    Eq.~\eqref{eq:reconcile}.
  \item \textbf{BCE + Hierarchical Hinge Penalty (Hinge):} Combines
    unconstrained BCE with the differentiable consistency loss of
    Eq.~\eqref{eq:hierloss}:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Hinge}}(\mathbf{P}, \mathbf{Y}) =
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(\mathbf{P}, \mathbf{Y}) +
    \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{hier}}(\mathbf{P}),
    \label{eq:total}
    \end{equation}
    where $\lambda > 0$ penalizes parent--child reversals on the raw outputs
    during training ($\lambda = 0.1$ in our protocol).
  \item \textbf{Max-Constraint Loss (MCLoss)~\cite{chmcnn}:} Applies binary
    cross-entropy to the \emph{reconciled} scores for negative targets ($y_{b,i}=0$)
    and to the positive-masked descendant maximum
    $\tilde{p}_{b,i} = \max_{j:\, R_{ij}=1} (y_{b,j} \cdot p_{b,j})$ for
    positive targets ($y_{b,i}=1$):
    \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{MC}}(\mathbf{P}, \mathbf{Y}) = -\frac{1}{B \cdot C}
    \sum_{b=1}^{B} \sum_{i=1}^{C} \left[
    y_{b,i} \log \tilde{p}_{b,i} + (1-y_{b,i}) \log(1-p_{b,i}^{\text{rec}})
    \right].
    \label{eq:mcloss}
    \end{equation}
    Note a critical property of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mcloss}: when an ancestor $i$ and
    its descendant $j$ are both positive ($y_{b,i}=y_{b,j}=1$) and $p_{b,j} >
    p_{b,i}$, the max-operator routes the positive gradient exclusively to the
    descendant $j$, leaving the raw ancestor unit $p_{b,i}$ without direct
    positive supervision and relying on inference-time reconciliation to
    restore $p_{b,i}^{\text{rec}}$.
\end{enumerate}

Crossing $\{ \text{BCE}, \text{Hinge}, \text{MCLoss} \} \times \{ \text{raw},
\text{reconciled} \}$ across all 27 datasets (Section~\ref{sec:ablation})
allows us to cleanly separate the training-time effect of hierarchical losses
from the inference-time effect of bottom-up reconciliation.

\subsection{Sibling-Repulsive GAE (\texttt{SR-GAE}), Hierarchical Multi-Label Contrastive Learning (\texttt{H-MLCL}), and Depth Calibration}
\label{sec:method_sr_gae_depth}

Beyond isolating the R-matrix constraint on standard classifiers, we introduce a
three-part geometric and contrastive framework that resolves the two structural
failure modes of ontology representation learning---horizontal sibling
collinearity and vertical depth probability decay---while preserving zero
hierarchy violations ($\text{GHV}=0$):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Sibling-Repulsive Graph Autoencoder (\texttt{SR-GAE}):} As we
    prove in Section~\ref{sec:sr_gae} via Bessel's inequality on
    $\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$, standard graph autoencoders trained to reconstruct
    $\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{I}$ force sibling classes sharing a parent onto nearly
    identical rays ($\cos \ge 0.999$). \texttt{SR-GAE} pairs an ego-preserving
    residual GCN encoder ($\mathbf{H}^{(l)} = \tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{H}^{(l-1)}
    \mathbf{W}_{\text{neigh}}^{(l)} + \alpha_{\text{skip}}\mathbf{H}^{(l-1)}
    \mathbf{W}_{\text{self}}^{(l)}$) with a squared angular repulsion penalty over
    sibling pairs $\mathcal{S} = \{(u,v) : u < v,\; \text{Pa}(u)\cap\text{Pa}(v)
    \neq \emptyset\}$, orthogonalizing sibling prototypes ($\cos \approx 0$) on a
    cone around their parent.
  \item \textbf{Hierarchical Multi-Label Contrastive Learning (\texttt{H-MLCL})
    and Contrastive $k$-NN Retrieval (\texttt{MLCL-KNN}):} Extending recent
    hierarchical multi-label contrastive frameworks~\cite{himulcone2022,hill2024,mlclknn2024}
    to featureless ontologies of arbitrary modality, we map hidden features
    $\mathbf{h}_b$ to the unit hypersphere $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_b =
    \text{normalize}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{cl}}\mathbf{h}_b) \in \mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ and
    jointly optimize:
    (a) a \emph{Sibling-Repulsive Prototype InfoNCE loss} against the frozen
    \texttt{SR-GAE} prototypes $\hat{\mathbf{Z}} \in \mathbb{S}^{C \times D}$ that
    up-weights inactive sibling prototypes $\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{Y}_b^+)$ by
    $\gamma_{\text{sib}} = 2.0$:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{proto-cl}}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_b, \mathbf{y}_b)
    &= -\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Y}_b^+|} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{Y}_b^+} \log
    \frac{\exp(\langle \hat{\mathbf{u}}_b, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_i \rangle / \tau)}
    {\sum_{c=1}^{C} \omega_{b,c}\,\exp(\langle \hat{\mathbf{u}}_b, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_c \rangle / \tau)},\\
    \omega_{b,c} &= 1 + (\gamma_{\text{sib}}-1)\mathbb{I}\bigl[c \in \mathcal{S}(\mathcal{Y}_b^+)\bigr],
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:proto_cl}
    \end{equation}
    and (b) an \emph{R-matrix Jaccard Instance Contrastive loss} over a
    first-in-first-out (FIFO) memory queue $\mathcal{Q} = \{(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_m,
    \mathbf{y}_m)\}_{m=1}^{M}$ ($M{=}128$) of normalized instance embeddings and
    ancestor-closed binary label vectors ($y_{m,i} = \max_{j: R_{ij}=1} y_{m,j}$),
    avoiding the $O(B^2)$ batch-size bottleneck of in-batch multi-label InfoNCE at
    the small batch size used here ($B = 4$):
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    w_{b,m} &= \frac{\langle \mathbf{y}_b, \mathbf{y}_m \rangle}
    {\|\mathbf{y}_b\|_1 + \|\mathbf{y}_m\|_1 - \langle \mathbf{y}_b, \mathbf{y}_m \rangle},\\
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{inst-cl}}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_b, \mathbf{y}_b)
    &= -\sum_{m:\, w_{b,m} > 0} \tilde{w}_{b,m} \log
    \frac{\exp(\langle \hat{\mathbf{u}}_b, \hat{\mathbf{u}}_m \rangle / \tau)}
    {\sum_{r \in \mathcal{Q}} \exp(\langle \hat{\mathbf{u}}_b, \hat{\mathbf{u}}_r \rangle / \tau)},
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:inst_cl}
    \end{equation}
    where $\tilde{w}_{b,m} = w_{b,m} / \sum_{m':\,w_{b,m'}>0} w_{b,m'}$. The normalized
    contrastive embedding $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_b$ directly drives the gated ontology
    residual $\sigma(\mathbf{h}_b\mathbf{W}_{\text{cls}}^\top + s \cdot
    \hat{\mathbf{u}}_b\hat{\mathbf{Z}}^\top)$ initialized at $s=0$. At inference,
    following~\cite{mlclknn2024}, we retrieve the top-$k$ cosine neighbors
    $\mathcal{N}_k(x)$ from the training contrastive bank
    $\{(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_n^{\text{tr}}, \mathbf{y}_n^{\text{tr}})\}_{n=1}^{N_{\text{tr}}}$
    with softmax weights $\alpha_n \propto \exp(\langle \hat{\mathbf{u}}(x),
    \hat{\mathbf{u}}_n^{\text{tr}} \rangle / \tau_{\text{knn}})$ to form
    $\mathbf{p}^{\text{knn}}(x) = \sum_{n \in \mathcal{N}_k(x)} \alpha_n
    \mathbf{y}_n^{\text{tr}}$ and blend $\mathbf{p}^{\text{blend}}(x) =
    (1-\beta)\mathbf{p}^{\text{net}}(x) + \beta\mathbf{p}^{\text{knn}}(x)$
    ($\beta \in [0, 0.15]$ selected on validation) prior to \texttt{BlockDiagonalR}
    reconciliation. Because every ancestor-closed training target satisfies
    $y_{n,p}^{\text{tr}} \ge y_{n,c}^{\text{tr}}$ for all $(c\to p)\in\mathcal{E}$
    and $\alpha_n \ge 0$, $\mathbf{p}^{\text{knn}}(x)$ is inherently hierarchy-consistent
    by convexity, and post-blending \texttt{BlockDiagonalR} propagation guarantees
    $\text{GHV}=0$.
  \item \textbf{Consistency-Preserving Depth-Level Logit Calibration (\texttt{DepthCal}):}
    To counter vertical probability decay at deep hierarchy levels ($\ell \ge 4$)
    without the $2C$-parameter overfitting of per-node Platt scaling,
    the \texttt{reconcile\_with\_depth\_offsets} routine of
    \texttt{BlockDiagonalR} learns $L$ scalar
    depth-level logit offsets $\boldsymbol{\delta} = (\delta_1, \ldots, \delta_L)$
    on the validation split prior to exact bottom-up max reconciliation
    (Section~\ref{sec:perlevel}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Reproducibility Protocol and Terminology}

Every experiment run produces a self-contained artifact directory storing raw
pre-reconciliation scores (\texttt{scores\_before\_postprocess.npz}),
reconciled scores (\texttt{scores\_final.npz}), evaluation metrics
(\texttt{metrics.json}), and full hyperparameter configurations
(\texttt{run-config.json}). Python, NumPy, and PyTorch random number generators
and deterministic cuDNN kernels are seeded explicitly (averaging seeds 0, 42,
and 123 across all main benchmark suites). Throughout the paper, we use
\textbf{path} for a directed child$\rightarrow$parent chain in $A$,
\textbf{dense R-matrix} for the explicit $C \times C$ closure matrix,
\textbf{R-matrix constraint} for the true-path consistency property,
\textbf{sparse R-matrix formulation} (\texttt{BlockDiagonalR}) for our
$O(C{+}E)$ graph-based layer, and \textbf{BFS levels} for breadth-first depth
from the root. We also evaluate post-hoc Platt scaling on validation data in
Section~\ref{sec:calib}, showing why raw probabilities with validation
threshold selection are preferable under extreme multi-label imbalance.

\subsection{Methods Compared}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Global} (Ours): MLP + R-matrix constraint.
    SPECTER2-base~\cite{specter2} embeddings for text (frozen or
    fine-tuned). Hidden dim per dataset from the registry.
  \item \textbf{SR-GAE + H-MLCL+H-KNN + DepthCal} (Ours): Our full method
    augmenting the global trunk with Sibling-Repulsive GAE ontology prototypes
    (\texttt{SR-GAE}), R-matrix Multi-Label Contrastive Learning with $k$-NN
    retrieval (\texttt{H-MLCL+H-KNN}), and consistency-preserving depth-level
    logit calibration (\texttt{DepthCal}) prior to \texttt{BlockDiagonalR}
    reconciliation (Section~\ref{sec:method_sr_gae_depth}).
  \item \textbf{Local}: Per-level MLPs, trained jointly, no R-matrix.
  \item \textbf{Tabular GBDT}: HistGradientBoosting One-vs-Rest
    (scikit-learn), one classifier per node with $\geq 5$ positives.
  \item \textbf{Tabular MLP}: Residual MLP + GlobalSigmoidHead,
    prevalence-weighted BCE.
  \item \textbf{HiAGM}~\cite{zhou2020hiagm}: Text encoder + label GCN.
  \item \textbf{HTCInfoMax}~\cite{htcinfomax}: HiAGM + mutual
    information maximization.
  \item \textbf{HMCN-F}~\cite{weihs2018hmc}: Feed-forward hierarchical
    network with level-wise propagation (FunCat).
  \item \textbf{C-HMCNN}~\cite{chmcnn}: Constraint layer that enforces
    coherence with the R-matrix.
  \item \textbf{Surj}~\cite{www2022ontology}: graph-autoencoder label
    embeddings scored by cosine similarity (WWW'22).  Its code was never
    released; we list its reported numbers and our reimplementation
    (Section~\ref{sec:surj}), but do not rank against them.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Training Details}

Text, FunCat and GO results are means over three seeds (0, 42, 123);
the inference-only GO ablation (Table~\ref{tab:sparse}) is a single
run. Optimizer: AdamW ($\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999$)
with weight decay $10^{-5}$. Text: SPECTER2-base (768-dim CLS-pooled,
256-token limit).
E2E fine-tuning: transformer LR $2\times10^{-5}$, classifier LR
$10^{-4}$, batch 8, up to 10 epochs with the epoch chosen on validation (WOS). GO: the FunCat protocol below;
the inference-only ablation uses LR $10^{-4}$, 20 epochs at batch 64
and dropout 0.3. GBDT: CPU-only (scikit-learn
HistGradientBoosting, max\_iter=100, no early stopping, one classifier
per node with $\geq 5$ positives). NVIDIA RTX 3060 (12~GB).

\textbf{FunCat protocol.} Features are mean-imputed and z-scored with
training-split statistics.  The global model is a 3-layer MLP (hidden
size per dataset from the registry, ReLU, dropout 0.7) trained with
MCLoss through the sparse R formulation, LR $10^{-4}$, batch 4.  The
number of epochs is chosen by early stopping on validation AUPRC
(patience 20, at most 200 epochs), and the model is then refit on
training + validation for that many epochs, the convention of HMCN and
C-HMCNN.  The decision threshold is chosen on validation and the test
split is used once.  The Gene Ontology runs of Table~\ref{tab:go_sota}
follow the same protocol.

\textbf{Metrics.} \textbf{Micro-F1} (primary for text) and
\textbf{Micro-AUPRC} (primary for FunCat, following literature
convention~\cite{www2022ontology}). For every benchmark, the decision
threshold is selected on validation ($[0.05,0.95]$ in steps of 0.05)
and the test split is used once.

\textbf{AAPD.} With frozen SPECTER2 + MLP + R-matrix (44,672 train,
100 epochs with patience 10, mean of 3 seeds), we achieve
\textbf{0.8232} $\pm$ 0.0003 Micro-F1 ($0.9071 \pm 0.0002$ AUPRC), and

(Table~\ref{tab:new_text}) on our re-split of the dataset.  Because the published baselines were measured
on the official 1,000-document SGM test set while our split holds out
11,168 documents, the figures are not directly comparable, and we
therefore do not claim a margin over them; on our split, a
frozen-embedding architecture is nonetheless competitive with
fine-tuned BERT models that use label GNNs
(HiAGM, HTCInfoMax, HiMatch). The R-matrix gain is small ($\Delta =
0.0003$) for a
structural reason: the constraint rewrites a node's score as the
maximum over its descendants, so only \emph{internal} nodes can
change---on AAPD that is the seven area nodes, while the remaining 54
annotated labels keep exactly their unconstrained scores. The constraint
still guarantees hierarchical consistency (no predicted sub-field can
have an unpredicted parent); it simply has little room to move
Micro-F1 on a hierarchy this shallow.

\textbf{EUR-Lex 57K.} With the standard 45,000/6,000/6,000 split
(4,271 dataset concepts; 4,520 hierarchy nodes, 4,519 evaluated after
excluding the synthetic root) and frozen SPECTER2 embeddings (100 epochs,
patience 10), our global model achieves $0.7888 \pm 0.0004$ Micro-F1
(0.7812 without reconciliation; AUPRC $0.8566 \pm 0.0006$), and
\texttt{SR-GAE + H-MLCL+H-KNN + DepthCal} raises it to \textbf{0.7893} $\pm$
0.0008 Micro-F1 (AUPRC $\mathbf{0.8575} \pm 0.0004$).  The R-matrix gain is +0.0076: the
EUROVOC hierarchy is deep (8 levels, 5,237 edges) and 29\% of the
evaluated labels are internal, but reconciliation changes only 10.7\%
of their scores (mean raise +0.010), so the F1 gain is small.  This is
consistent with the pattern that the gain tracks how many internal
scores reconciliation actually moves, not the depth alone.  The
256-token truncation leaves the main body outside the window; moving it
ahead of the header and recitals (so that it is encoded) lowers Micro-F1
to 0.7830, so we keep the release's order.

\textbf{RCV1-V2 (LYRL2004 open-route diagnostic).} Because raw Reuters prose
requires a restricted NIST distribution agreement, we evaluated the publicly
available LYRL2004 benchmark release ($23{,}149$ train+validation / $781{,}265$
test documents across all 103 topics; 104 nodes with root, depth 4), which
provides stemmed, stopword-removed tokens ordered by document frequency rather
than running text. Feeding these stemmed token sequences into frozen SPECTER2
confirming our $3 \times 2$ decomposition on a 781K-document test set:
bottom-up \texttt{BlockDiagonalR} reconciliation improves Micro-AUPRC across all
three objectives (BCE $0.7603 \to 0.7629$, Hinge $0.7617 \to \mathbf{0.7633}$,
MCLoss $0.7315 \to 0.7621$) and lifts MCLoss Micro-F1 from $0.6642 \to
\mathbf{0.6903}$ while eliminating all hierarchy violations ($\text{GHV}:
0.0959 \to 0.0000$). Because pretrained subword transformers suffer a
distributional mismatch on stemmed frequency-sorted bag-of-words inputs, we
report RCV1-V2 here as a large-scale structural verification rather than a
like-for-like comparison against raw-prose BERT baselines.

\subsection{Reproducing Surj}
\label{sec:surj}

Surj~\cite{www2022ontology} is the only published model above the
constraint-based methods on FunCat and GO, by 5.5--29\% AUPRC over C-HMCNN (up
to 31\% over the stronger baseline).  Its code
was announced but never released, so we reimplemented it
(\texttt{surj\_repro/} in the repository) from the paper alone, on top of
the components it says it reuses.

\textbf{What the paper fixes, and what we chose.}  Surj ``adopts the code
provided by'' C-HMCNN for preprocessing and evaluation: we import
C-HMCNN's parser unmodified, and the same pipeline reproduces C-HMCNN's
published FunCat numbers to within 0.003 AUPRC.  Label embeddings come
from a two-layer GCN graph autoencoder~\cite{kipf2016vgae} trained on
the ontology without node features; samples are mapped by three fully
connected layers to the embedding space, compared to every label by
cosine similarity, and passed through one more fully connected layer
and a sigmoid, trained with BCE at learning rate $10^{-3}$.  Batch size,
epochs, dropout, layer sizes, embedding dimension and whether
C-HMCNN's hierarchy constraint was kept are not reported; following the
paper (``optimized with the validation set'', ``consistent across
datasets''), we chose them by a staged search of 25 configurations on
FunCat validation AUPRC (seed 0; the test split never scored).  The
search kept batch 4, dropout 0.7, hidden size 512, embedding dimension
16 and, notably, C-HMCNN's max-constraint module with MCLoss: without
it the model produces $10^4$--$10^5$ hierarchy violations per dataset,
while Surj reports none.  The validation AUPRC of all 25 configurations
spans 0.187--0.217, far less than the gap below.

\subsection{Autoencoders in Our Model and the Sibling-Repulsive GAE}

not, they should help our model too.  We tried three ways of adding an

\begin{itemize}

    mapped to a shared latent space and decoded to the labels, with
    reconstruction and latent matching weighted by

\subsubsection{Diagnosing and Resolving Sibling Collinearity Collapse: SR-GAE}
\label{sec:sr_gae}

Why does a pure cosine-similarity classifier over standard Graph Autoencoder
(GAE) embeddings fail on hierarchical taxonomies? In
Surj~\cite{www2022ontology}, a two-layer GCN encoder
$\mathbf{Z} = \tilde{\mathbf{A}}\,\text{ReLU}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_0)\mathbf{W}_1$
is trained to reconstruct the adjacency matrix $\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{I}$ via an
inner-product decoder $\sigma(\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{Z}^\top)$ with positive edge
weight $w_+ = (C^2 - P)/P \gg 1$. Two structural mechanisms in taxonomic trees
and DAGs interact to cause severe \emph{sibling collinearity collapse}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Bessel / Cauchy--Schwarz Bound:} Let a parent node
    $p$ have $K$ children $\{c_1, \ldots, c_K\}$ with unit-norm embeddings
    $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_p, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{c_i} \in \mathbb{S}^{D-1}$. By
    Bessel's inequality,
    $\sum_{i=1}^{K} \langle \hat{\mathbf{z}}_p, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{c_i} \rangle^2 \le \|\hat{\mathbf{z}}_p\|_2^2 = 1$,
    so when the $K$ parent--child cosines are equal (the symmetric case), their
    common value is upper-bounded by:
    \begin{equation}
    \cos(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_p, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{c_i}) \le \frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}.
    \label{eq:parseval_bound}
    \end{equation}
    Because the weighted GAE objective drives
    $\cos(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_p, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{c_i}) \to 1$ along every
    parent--child edge, the equal-cosine bound is violated and the sibling
    vectors $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{c_1}, \ldots, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{c_K}$ are driven
    onto the same ray ($\cos(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{c_i}, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{c_j}) \to 1$).
  \item \textbf{Intra- vs.\ Inter-Subtree Negative Asymmetry:} In a taxonomy
    with $S$ major subtrees, over $(1 - 1/S)$ of non-edge pairs $(u,v) \notin
    \mathcal{E}$ lie in \emph{different} subtrees. Standard GAE satisfies the
    negative reconstruction term by pushing distinct subtrees apart while
    two-layer Laplacian smoothing ($\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^2$) averages sibling
    nodes that share the same parent neighborhood.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome this geometric bottleneck without requiring external text descriptions
or incurring the $O(B^2)$ per-batch overhead of instance-level hierarchical
contrastive learning~\cite{himulcone2022,hcl4qc2023,hill2024},
we introduce the \textbf{Sibling-Repulsive Graph Autoencoder (\texttt{SR-GAE})},
which combines two modifications in \texttt{hmc-torch}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Ego-Preserving Residual GCN Encoder:} Each layer augments
    neighborhood smoothing with a linear skip connection that preserves each
    node's ego-identity:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{H}^{(l)} = \tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{H}^{(l-1)}\mathbf{W}_{\text{neigh}}^{(l)}
    + \alpha_{\text{skip}}\,\mathbf{H}^{(l-1)}\mathbf{W}_{\text{self}}^{(l)},
    \quad \alpha_{\text{skip}} = 0.5.
    \label{eq:res_gcn}
    \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Sibling-Repulsive Angular Hinge Objective:} Let
    $\mathcal{S} = \{(u,v) : u < v,\; \text{Pa}(u) \cap \text{Pa}(v) \neq \emptyset\}$
    denote the set of sibling pairs sharing at least one parent. We penalize
    excessive cosine alignment between siblings on the unit hypersphere:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{SR-GAE}}(\mathbf{Z}) =
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{GAE}}(\mathbf{Z}, \mathbf{A}+\mathbf{I}) +
    \lambda_{\text{sib}} \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}
    \sum_{(u,v) \in \mathcal{S}} \max\bigl(0,\; \cos(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_u, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_v) - m_{\text{sib}}\bigr)^2,
    \label{eq:sr_gae}
    \end{equation}
    with $\lambda_{\text{sib}} = 5.0$ and margin $m_{\text{sib}} = 0.0$.
    Eq.~\eqref{eq:sr_gae} arranges siblings orthogonally ($\cos \approx 0$) on a cone
    around their parent vector ($\cos(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_p, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{c_i})
    \approx 0.53$), respecting the spherical bound of Eq.~\eqref{eq:parseval_bound}.
\end{enumerate}
Furthermore, instead of Surj's $C \times C$ dense post-cosine layer
(which adds $C^2$ parameters and blocks initial gradient flow when gated by a
zero scalar), we introduce \texttt{SrGaeHybrid}, a parameter-efficient low-rank
residual $\sigma(\mathbf{h}\mathbf{W}_{\text{cls}}^\top + s \cdot
(\mathbf{h}\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}})\hat{\mathbf{Z}}^\top)$ initialized with
$\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}} = \mathbf{0}$ and $s = 5.0$, which starts identically
to the baseline MLP at epoch 0 while propagating non-zero gradients to
$\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}$ from the first batch. Finally, in
\texttt{SrGaeMlclHybrid} (Section~\ref{sec:method_sr_gae_depth}), we train the
unit-norm projection $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_b =
\text{normalize}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{cl}}\mathbf{h}_b)$ directly with our
\textbf{Hierarchical Multi-Label Contrastive Loss (\texttt{H-MLCL})}
(combining the sibling-boosted prototype InfoNCE loss of Eq.~\eqref{eq:proto_cl}
and the FIFO memory-queue R-matrix Jaccard instance contrastive loss of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:inst_cl}), feed $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_b$ into the gated cosine residual
$s \cdot \hat{\mathbf{u}}_b\hat{\mathbf{Z}}^\top$ ($s$ initialized at $0$), and
at inference time apply validation-selected \textbf{Contrastive $k$-NN Retrieval
(\texttt{MLCL-KNN})} prior to \texttt{BlockDiagonalR} reconciliation.

\subsection{Per-Level Analysis and Consistency-Preserving Depth Calibration}
\label{sec:perlevel}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig5_per_level_f1.pdf}
\caption{F1 by hierarchy depth level across 4 representative datasets.
F1 falls moderately with depth on the text hierarchies (1.3--1.6$\times$
from the root to the deepest populated level) and by an order of
magnitude on the FunCat and GO hierarchies; the GO panel is not
monotonic, and its sparsest levels are effectively unlearnable.}
\label{fig:perlevel}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:perlevel} shows F1 broken down by hierarchy depth. On
the text hierarchies the decrease is monotonic and moderate (ArXiv
0.93 $\rightarrow$ 0.59, WOS 0.86 $\rightarrow$ 0.65).  On FunCat and
GO the drop is an order of magnitude larger, and it is \emph{not}
monotonic: the GO hierarchy's highest level---a single node---scores
near zero while the level beneath it reaches 0.68, and the deepest
levels hold only a handful of labels each.  Deep, sparsely populated
levels are therefore both the hardest and the noisiest regime, and the
one where bottom-up reconciliation changes the most predictions.

\textbf{Combining Horizontal (\texttt{SR-GAE}) and Vertical (Depth-Level)
Geometry.} While \texttt{SR-GAE} resolves the \emph{horizontal} collinearity
among sibling nodes within the same depth level, Figure~\ref{fig:perlevel}
reveals a complementary \emph{vertical} calibration gap across levels: applying
a single global decision threshold $t$ penalizes deep leaf classes ($\ell \ge
4$) whose base rates and predicted probabilities are systematically lower than
those of upper-level ancestors. Conversely, per-node Platt calibration
(Section~\ref{sec:calib}) fits $2C$ parameters and overfits rare leaves. To
correct vertical score decay without overfitting or violating the true-path
rule, we introduce \textbf{Consistency-Preserving Depth-Level Logit Calibration}
(\texttt{reconcile\_with\_depth\_offsets} in \texttt{BlockDiagonalR}), which
learns $L$ scalar logit offsets $\boldsymbol{\delta} = (\delta_1,
\ldots, \delta_L)$---one per populated hierarchy level $\ell$, estimated by
coordinate search on the validation split---prior to exact bottom-up
reconciliation:
\begin{equation}
p_i^{\text{depth-rec}} = \max_{j:\, R_{ij}=1}
\sigma\bigl(\text{logit}(p_j) + \delta_{\ell_j}\bigr).
\label{eq:depth_cal}
\end{equation}
Because Eq.~\eqref{eq:depth_cal} pools all validation instances across all nodes
at depth $\ell$ ($L$ parameters instead of $2C = 1{,}000$; $L{=}6$ on FunCat,
up to 11 on GO, and 8 on EUR-Lex) and applies
\texttt{BlockDiagonalR} propagation afterward, it guarantees zero hierarchy
violations for any offset vector $\boldsymbol{\delta}$. Across all 10 FunCat
benchmarks in the ablation of Table~\ref{tab:sr_gae}, combining \texttt{SR-GAE}/\texttt{H-MLCL} and
depth-level logit calibration improves deep-level test F1 ($\ell \ge 4$) on
\textbf{10 out of 10 datasets} (mean $0.1212 \to 0.1370$, $+13.0\%$ relative),
lifting \texttt{spo\_FUN} from $0.0574 \to 0.1009$ ($+75.8\%$),
\texttt{church\_FUN} from $0.0587 \to 0.0845$ ($+44.0\%$), \texttt{derisi\_FUN}
from $0.0664 \to 0.0853$ ($+28.5\%$), and \texttt{pheno\_FUN} from $0.0389 \to
0.0455$ ($+17.0\%$), while simultaneously improving global test Micro-F1 on
\textbf{10 out of 10 FunCat datasets} (mean $0.2903 \to 0.2945$, Wilcoxon
$p = 0.0020$, including $+0.84\text{ pp}$ on \texttt{seq\_FUN}, $+0.76\text{ pp}$
on \texttt{pheno\_FUN}, and $+0.74\text{ pp}$ on \texttt{cellcycle\_FUN}). Across
the 9 Gene Ontology datasets, it improves global test Micro-F1 on \textbf{8 out
of 9 datasets} (mean $0.4117 \to \mathbf{0.4171}$, Wilcoxon $p = 0.0039$, up to
$+1.00\text{ pp}$ on \texttt{eisen\_GO} and $+0.94\text{ pp}$ on
\texttt{gasch1\_GO}) and lifts deep Level-5 test F1 ($\ell{=}5$, $\sim 1{,}070$
classes) from $0.1305 \to \mathbf{0.1620}$ ($+24.1\%$ relative, improving 8/9
datasets, including $+121.5\%$ on \texttt{spo\_GO}, $+76.0\%$ on
\texttt{derisi\_GO}, and $+55.1\%$ on \texttt{gasch1\_GO}), while also improving
\texttt{imclef07d} ($0.8370 \to 0.8461$), \texttt{diatoms} ($0.7003 \to 0.7039$),
\texttt{arxiv} ($0.7221 \to 0.7248$), and \texttt{wos} ($0.7411 \to 0.7455$).

\textbf{Training vs.\ Inference Decomposition (Q1).} Three key findings emerge
from Tables~\ref{tab:ablation}--\ref{tab:decomp_family} and
Figures~\ref{fig:decomp}--\ref{fig:decomp_27}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Inference-time reconciliation is universally beneficial.} For
    plain BCE-trained models across all 27 datasets, applying bottom-up max
    reconciliation at inference time improves Micro-AUPRC on \textbf{26 of 27
    datasets} (median $\Delta = +0.0016$, interquartile range $[+0.0008,
    +0.0044]$, paired two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test $p = 2.1 \times
    10^{-7}$) and improves Micro-F1 on \textbf{25 of 27 datasets} (median
    $\Delta = +0.0003$, IQR $[+0.0001, +0.0017]$, $p = 1.3 \times 10^{-6}$).
  \item \textbf{Training-time consistency penalties do not drive the gain.}
    Adding the hierarchical hinge penalty without reconciliation
    ($\text{Hinge}_{\text{raw}}$ vs.\ $\text{BCE}_{\text{raw}}$) yields a near-zero
    median difference across all 27 datasets (Table~\ref{tab:decomp_family}),
    and once reconciliation is applied at inference time, $\text{BCE}_{\text{rec}}$,
    $\text{Hinge}_{\text{rec}}$, and $\text{MCLoss}_{\text{rec}}$ converge to
    virtually identical performance across all four families (e.g., $0.2429$,
    $0.2429$, and $0.2439$ AUPRC on FunCat; $0.4080$, $0.4083$, and $0.4092$ on
    Gene Ontology).
  \item \textbf{Pre-reconciliation collapse under MCLoss.} Evaluating MCLoss on
    raw outputs ($\text{MCLoss}_{\text{raw}}$) reveals a severe degradation
    prior to reconciliation ($-0.0168$ AUPRC on FunCat, $-0.0383$ on GO, and
    $-0.0708$ on Text relative to $\text{MCLoss}_{\text{rec}}$). As analyzed in
    Eq.~\eqref{eq:mcloss}, when an ancestor and descendant are co-positive and
    the descendant already scores higher, the max operator masks the ancestor's
    direct gradient, leaving internal nodes under-trained and wholly dependent
    on inference-time reconciliation.
\end{enumerate}

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_structure.pdf}
\caption{Structural predictors of reconciliation gain ($\Delta$ Micro-AUPRC from
$\text{BCE}_{\text{raw}}$ to $\text{BCE}_{\text{rec}}$ across all 27 datasets).
\textbf{Left:} Static fraction of positive annotations located on internal
nodes shows no correlation with constraint gain (Spearman $\rho = -0.07$,
$p = 0.721$). \textbf{Right:} Average score raise per evaluated node induced by
bottom-up propagation correlates strongly with the AUPRC gain (Spearman
$\rho = 0.49$, $p = 0.009$).}
\label{fig:structure}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Structural Predictors of Constraint Gain (Q3).} Why does reconciliation
produce large gains on some benchmarks---such as \texttt{diatoms} ($+0.0114$
F1, $+0.0110$ AUPRC) and \texttt{EUR-Lex 57K} ($+0.0076$ F1, $+0.0083$
AUPRC)---while having a modest $+0.0003$ to $+0.0017$ effect on others? A
natural hypothesis is that datasets with a larger share of positive annotations
on internal (non-leaf) nodes benefit more, because reconciliation can only
modify internal node scores. Figure~\ref{fig:structure} (left) refutes this
static hypothesis: across the 27 datasets, the Spearman rank correlation
between the fraction of positive labels on internal nodes and $\Delta$ AUPRC is
$\rho = -0.07$ ($p = 0.721$). In Gene Ontology and FunCat, over 80\% of
positive annotations reside on internal nodes, yet high base prevalence allows
the unconstrained MLP to already predict high ancestor probabilities. Instead,
Figure~\ref{fig:structure} (right) demonstrates that the reconciliation gain is
governed by the \textbf{average score raise per evaluated node},
$\frac{1}{N \cdot C} \sum_{b,i} (p_{b,i}^{\text{rec}} - p_{b,i})$, which
captures how frequently and strongly specific descendant detections correct
under-confident ancestors ($\rho = 0.49$, $p = 0.009$). This regime occurs when
fine-grained leaf or mid-level classes have distinctive input features (such as
morphological contour descriptors in \texttt{diatoms} or specific legal
 descriptors in \texttt{EUR-Lex 57K}) while their broader parent categories are
heterogeneous and harder to classify directly.

\subsection{Ablation: Platt Calibration on FunCat}
\label{sec:calib}

Applying Platt scaling (per-node logistic regression fitted on
validation) \textbf{decreased} raw GBDT AUPRC from 0.219 to 0.212 on
average over the 8 FunCat datasets with published baselines; the reconciled
GBDT in Table~\ref{tab:funcat} is 0.226.  Platt scaling is monotonic per node, so it cannot change the
ranking within a class; Micro-AUPRC, however, pools scores across
classes, and pulling every node toward its prevalence ($<5\%$ for most
leaf nodes) reorders them.  We recommend raw scores over calibration
for highly imbalanced HMC.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Where Hierarchical Constraints Excel---and Where They Do Not}

Our 27-dataset evaluation reveals a nuanced picture of how taxonomic
constraints interact with learned representations:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Inference-time reconciliation vs.\ training-time losses.}
    Across all four domain families, the benefit of the R-matrix constraint
    arises almost entirely at inference time via bottom-up max propagation
    (Eq.~\eqref{eq:reconcile}), rather than from training-time consistency
    penalties (Eq.~\eqref{eq:hierloss}) or max-constraint loss
    (Eq.~\eqref{eq:mcloss}). When a base classifier detects a specific leaf or
    mid-level concept with higher confidence than its broader ancestor,
    bottom-up reconciliation corrects the ancestor score and guarantees zero
    hierarchy violations, improving Micro-AUPRC on 26/27 datasets ($p = 2.1
    \times 10^{-7}$).
  \item \textbf{When the gain is large vs.\ modest.} Reconciliation provides
    its largest gains on hierarchies where specific descendant classes have
    distinctive input signals while parent categories are heterogeneous---such
    as \texttt{diatoms} ($+0.0114$ F1, $+0.0110$ AUPRC) and \texttt{EUR-Lex
    57K} ($+0.0076$ F1, $+0.0083$ AUPRC). On FunCat and Gene Ontology, where
    ancestor classes already have high base prevalence, unconstrained MLPs
    predict relatively high parent probabilities on their own, so
    reconciliation yields a smaller but still positive gain ($+0.0015$ to
    $+0.0016$ F1 on FunCat, Table~\ref{tab:ablation}; $+0.0016$ mean AUPRC on
    GO, Table~\ref{tab:decomp_family}). As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:structure},
    the average score raise per node ($\rho = 0.49$, $p = 0.009$)---not static
    internal-node label density ($\rho = -0.07$)---predicts this effect.
  \item \textbf{Scalability to large DAGs.} While the dense $C \times C$
    R-matrix requires $O(C^2)$ storage and $O(B \cdot C^2)$ batch intermediates,
    our sparse \texttt{BlockDiagonalR} formulation (Section~\ref{sec:sparse})
    reduces structural memory to $O(C{+}E)$ and batch propagation to $O(B \cdot
    E)$ with zero hierarchy violations. On the 9 Gene Ontology DAGs (up to
    4,134 nodes / 4,130 evaluated and 5,839 edges), sparse R matches C-HMCNN and HMCN-F (0.418
    vs.\ 0.420 and 0.422 AUPRC on the 8 benchmarked datasets, and \textbf{0.421}
    when combined with \texttt{SR-GAE + H-MLCL+H-KNN}, beating C-HMCNN on 5/8
    datasets) while running at batch sizes where dense R exhausts GPU memory.
  \item \textbf{Ontology geometry, hierarchical contrastive learning, and vertical
    depth calibration complement bottom-up reconciliation.}
    Surj~\cite{www2022ontology} reported 5.5--29\% AUPRC improvements over
    C-HMCNN (up to 31\% over the stronger baseline) using graph-autoencoder label embeddings, but
    released no code. Our faithful reimplementation on C-HMCNN's own pipeline
    does not reproduce those gains (Section~\ref{sec:surj}), and our theoretical
    analysis (Section~\ref{sec:method_sr_gae_depth}) explains why: standard GAE
    link reconstruction forces $\cos(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_p, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{c_i})
    \to 1$, which by Bessel's inequality ($\le 1/\sqrt{K}$) collapses sibling
    embeddings onto a single ray (median cosine up to 0.9995). Combining our
    Sibling-Repulsive Graph Autoencoder (\texttt{SR-GAE}) with Hierarchical
    Multi-Label Contrastive Learning and $k$-NN retrieval (\texttt{H-MLCL+H-KNN})
    resolves both horizontal sibling collapse and vertical probability decay,
    strictly improving 3-seed test Micro-AUPRC over the matched baseline on
    \textbf{27/27 datasets} (Wilcoxon $p = 1.5 \times 10^{-8}$; 26/27 strict wins
    in controlled single-stage ablation over 3 seeds, $p = 4.5 \times 10^{-8}$);
    adding consistency-preserving depth calibration (\texttt{DepthCal}) lifts
    test F1 on \textbf{26/27 datasets} ($p = 2.5 \times 10^{-6}$; 26/27 strict,
    27/27 non-decreasing, $p = 3.0 \times 10^{-8}$ in 3-seed controlled ablation)
    as well as deep-level F1 ($+13.0\%$ relative on FunCat and $+24.1\%$ relative
    on GO Level 5) with zero hierarchy violations.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Practical Guidance for Knowledge-Constrained HMC}

Based on our empirical findings across 27 benchmarks, we offer five actionable
recommendations for practitioners designing knowledge-based HMC systems:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Default to unconstrained BCE training + inference-time
    reconciliation.} Plain BCE combined with bottom-up max reconciliation
    matches or exceeds Hinge and MCLoss on reconciled metrics while preserving
    well-calibrated pre-reconciliation probabilities on internal nodes.
  \item \textbf{Never deploy MCLoss without inference-time reconciliation.}
    Because MCLoss masks positive gradients on ancestors whenever a positive
    descendant scores higher, raw MCLoss outputs suffer severe degradation on
    internal nodes (Table~\ref{tab:decomp_family}); reconciliation is mandatory
    at prediction time.
  \item \textbf{Use edge-indexed sparse propagation (\texttt{BlockDiagonalR})
    whenever $C > 1{,}000$.} Sparse bottom-up propagation over
    child$\rightarrow$parent edges is mathematically equivalent to the dense
    ancestor closure but eliminates the $O(B \cdot C^2)$ memory wall on large
    ontologies such as GO and EUR-Lex.
  \item \textbf{Avoid independent per-node Platt calibration; use $L$-parameter
    depth calibration (\texttt{DepthCal}) prior to reconciliation.} Per-class
    logistic calibrators ($2C$ parameters) overfit rare leaves and distort
    cross-class pooling in Micro-AUPRC (Section~\ref{sec:calib}), whereas
    fitting $L$ per-depth logit offsets before \texttt{BlockDiagonalR}
    propagation corrects vertical probability decay and preserves zero hierarchy
    violations.
  \item \textbf{Enforce sibling repulsion (\texttt{SR-GAE}) and R-matrix
    contrastive learning (\texttt{H-MLCL+H-KNN}) when using ontology embeddings.}
    Never use standard unweighted or positive-weighted GAE inner-product
    reconstruction alone for cosine classification on trees or DAGs; add
    residual ego-skip connections, sibling angular repulsion, and R-matrix
    Jaccard contrastive alignment, especially in the Rankin--Welch regime
    ($K_{\max} > D$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations and Threats to Validity}

We structure the limitations of our study around four standard validity
dimensions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Internal validity.} All models, ablations, and threshold sweeps
    follow a strict validation-only protocol in which test sets are evaluated
    exactly once. On FunCat and GO, we follow the established C-HMCNN protocol
    (early stopping on validation AUPRC followed by refitting on
    train$+$validation for the selected epoch count). While refitting increases
    training sample size, we apply the identical protocol across all compared
    objectives (BCE, Hinge, MCLoss, and GAE) and fixed seeds (0, 42, 123).
  \item \textbf{External validity.} Our benchmark suite covers 27 primary
    datasets across four families (scientific/legal text, yeast functional
    genomics in FunCat and GO, microscopy, email, and medical imaging), plus an
    open-route structural diagnostic on RCV1-V2 (LYRL2004 stemmed tokens).
    However, raw Reuters prose for RCV1-V2 requires a restricted NIST license,
    and our biological experiments rely on pre-extracted tabular and expression
    features rather than end-to-end raw protein sequence encoders (such as
    ESM-2).
  \item \textbf{Construct validity.} Following community standards, we report
    Micro-F1 as the primary metric for text classification and Micro-AUPRC for
    tabular/genomic HMC. Both micro-averaged metrics weight classes by
    instance prevalence; rare leaf classes contribute less to the aggregate
    score than frequent internal classes, which we mitigate by analyzing
    per-level performance in Section~\ref{sec:perlevel}. Additionally, because
    the original Surj codebase was never released, our reproducibility audit
    evaluates a faithful reimplementation from the published equations and
    hyperparameters rather than the authors' original binary.
  \item \textbf{Statistical conclusion validity.} Multi-dataset comparisons on
    FunCat ($N{=}8$ datasets with published baselines, $N{=}10$ total) and GO
    ($N{=}8$ with baselines, $N{=}9$ total) have bounded statistical power under
    non-parametric Friedman--Nemenyi tests (critical difference $\text{CD} =
    2.16$ at $\alpha = 0.05$). Conversely, our primary decomposition test pools
    all $N{=}27$ datasets, providing high statistical power for the paired
    Wilcoxon signed-rank test ($p = 2.1 \times 10^{-7}$ for AUPRC and
    $p = 1.3 \times 10^{-6}$ for Micro-F1).
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

We presented a unified methodological and empirical study of scalable R-matrix
reconciliation, sibling-repulsive ontology graphs, and hierarchical multi-label
contrastive learning across 27 datasets spanning four domain families. Our
principal conclusions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Inference-time reconciliation drives the constraint gain (Q1):}
    A $3 \times 2$ factorial evaluation of BCE, Hinge, and MCLoss with and
    without inference-time reconciliation demonstrates that bottom-up
    reconciliation improves Micro-AUPRC on 26/27 datasets ($p = 2.1\times
    10^{-7}$) and Micro-F1 on 25/27 datasets ($p = 1.3\times 10^{-6}$), whereas
    training-time consistency losses alone do not improve over plain BCE and
    cause pre-reconciliation score collapse under MCLoss.
  \item \textbf{Exact $O(C{+}E)$ scaling to large DAGs (Q2):} Our sparse
    R-matrix formulation (\texttt{BlockDiagonalR}) enforces the exact
    ancestor-closure constraint over graph edges with $>1{,}400\times$ less
    structural memory than the dense $O(C^2)$ R-matrix. Combined with
    \texttt{SR-GAE}+\texttt{H-MLCL+H-KNN}, it matches C-HMCNN on Gene Ontology
    (0.421 vs.\ 0.420 AUPRC, winning on 5/8 datasets) while 0.001 below HMCN-F
    (0.422), is statistically tied with C-HMCNN on FunCat (0.263 vs.\ 0.264
    AUPRC) and above HMCN-F (0.261), and improves cross-domain benchmarks
    (0.859 vs.\ 0.849 AUPRC); a fine-tuned SPECTER2 (globalE2E) reaches 0.8704
    Micro-F1 on Web of Science (matching our 3-seed re-execution of HPT at
    $0.8691 \pm 0.0023$).
  \item \textbf{Structural predictors of constraint utility (Q3):} Constraint
    gains correlate significantly with the average score raise on internal
    nodes ($\rho = 0.49$, $p = 0.009$) rather than static internal-node label
    density ($\rho = -0.07$, $p = 0.721$).
  \item \textbf{Sibling-repulsive ontology geometry, hierarchical contrastive
    learning, and depth calibration (Q4):} Auditing Surj~\cite{www2022ontology}
    reveals that its reported figures cannot be verified and that standard
    ontology GAEs suffer from sibling collinear collapse (median cosine up to
    0.9995, consistent with a Bessel-type $1/\sqrt{K}$ bound). Our proposed
    \texttt{SR-GAE} and \texttt{H-MLCL+H-KNN} reduce the median sibling cosine
    to $\le 0.038$ and strictly improve 3-seed test Micro-AUPRC over the matched
    baseline on 27/27 benchmarks (Wilcoxon $p = 1.5 \times 10^{-8}$; 26/27 strict
    wins in 3-seed controlled ablation, $p = 4.5 \times 10^{-8}$); adding
    \texttt{DepthCal} lifts 3-seed test F1 on 26/27 benchmarks ($p = 2.5 \times
    10^{-6}$; 26/27 strict, 27/27 non-decreasing, $p = 3.0 \times 10^{-8}$ in
    controlled ablation) as well as deep-level F1 by $+13.0\%$ on FunCat and
    $+24.1\%$ on GO Level 5 with zero hierarchy violations.
\end{itemize}

Future work will investigate coupling \texttt{SR-GAE} and \texttt{H-MLCL+H-KNN}
with end-to-end biological sequence backbones (such as ESM-2), rich textual
node definitions in the Rankin--Welch packing regime ($K_{\max} > D$), and
differentiable tree ensembles with in-training reconciliation.
Source code and experiment manifests for \texttt{hmc-torch} are publicly
available on GitHub (\url{https://github.com/Sette/hmc-torch}) and PyPI
(\url{https://pypi.org/project/hmc-torch/}).
